Why is Iron Important?

Iron is an essential mineral that mainly transports oxygen through the blood and supports cellular energy production. Around 70% of the body's iron is found in hemoglobin and myoglobin. Iron forms a core part of hemoglobin in red blood cells to carry oxygen from the lungs to tissues. It also helps make myoglobin, which stores and delivers oxygen in muscle cells. In addition, iron powers enzymes and proteins in the electron transport chain that help cells convert nutrients into usable energy (ATP), helps produce neurotransmitter to support cognitive function, and it is needed for the immune body. That makes iron play a crucial part for the human body.

How Does the Body Absorb Iron?

The body absorbs dietary iron mainly in the duodenum and upper jejunum of the small intestine, taking in only about 1 to 2 mg per day despite higher amounts in food.

Heme vs Non-Heme

  • Heme Iron (Animal Sources): Already in a bioavailable form, meaning it doesn’t need much processing and is relatively easily absorbed. One of the best direct, highly absorbable sources, Grass-Fed Desiccated Beef Blood, delivers heme iron in its most effective form.
  • Non-Heme Iron (Plant Sources / Suplement) Has to be converted into a more absorbable state before it can pass through the intestinal lining.Makes up most dietary iron (often in the ferric, or Fe3+, form).

The Arbsoption Process

Once iron leaves the stomach, it enters the first two sections of the small intestine: duodenum and jejenum. This is where absorption truly begins.
